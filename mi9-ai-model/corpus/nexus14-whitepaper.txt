 1. The Question and the Answer
 We were handed a deceptively simple mandate: find out whether an AI system can be made substantially
 smarter without absorbing the entire internet, and whether the result can run locally on a consumer
 computer. The industry default answer is no on both counts. Frontier capability is widely believed to
 require frontier-scale pretraining over trillions of scraped tokens, which in turn requires datacenter-class
 hardware. This whitepaper reviews the published evidence of the last two years, works through the
 arithmetic, and reaches a different conclusion: the two halves of that belief are separable, and one of
 them is already false.

 The answer, stated once and then defended for the rest of the document: stop memorizing the internet
 into the weights, and instead build a small model that reasons, retrieves, verifies, and adapts.
 Intelligence decomposes. Knowledge is a warehouse problem, and warehouses are cheap on disk.
 Reasoning is a compute problem, and the 2025 evidence shows reasoning is trainable with reinforcement
 learning on a few hundred thousand verifiable tasks rather than trillions of web tokens. Verification can
 be outsourced to a code executor that never forgets how to check an answer. Adaptation can run nightly
 on the user’s own machine, on the user’s own tasks, with LoRA adapters instead of full retraining. None
 of these four components needs the whole internet; only the warehouse does, and a warehouse fits on a
 laptop disk.

 This position is not speculative futurism. Every ingredient already exists in the open literature with

 engineering task ahead of us is composition, not invention.

 1.1 Four design principles
 Principle 1, cognition-first: spend the parameter and compute budget on a strong reasoning core trained
 with RL on verifiable tasks, not on a maximally knowledgeable base. Reasoning transfers across domains;
 encyclopedic recall does not. Principle 2, knowledge-retrieved: externalize world knowledge into a
 curated local corpus plus live retrieval, so facts cost kilobytes on disk instead of gigabytes of weights and
 never go stale silently. Principle 3, verification-grounded: never let the model do arithmetic, code, or
 logic unaided when a sandboxed executor can check it; precision is a service, not a learned skill.
 Principle 4, experience-fed: close the loop on-device, letting the system generate its own verifiable
 practice tasks each night and distill its validated wins into swappable adapters. Sutton and Silver call this
 the era of experience: data generated by the agent, rewarded by the environment, unbounded by the fixed
 stock of human text.

 The document follows a deliberate arc. Chapter 2 frames the problem quantitatively: the data wall, the
 separation of knowledge from reasoning, and the true arithmetic of a consumer GPU. Chapter 3 surveys
 the full landscape of techniques that buy intelligence without web-scale data, with published numbers
 attached to every claim. Chapter 4 synthesizes those levers into a concrete reference architecture,
 Mi9-Nexus, sized for an entry-class consumer GPU. Chapter 5 works the scaling math and training
 budgets. Chapter 6 lists the ways this thesis can fail, because a roadmap without failure modes is a
 brochure. Chapter 7 turns the whole thing into a phased 24-month build plan with measurable gates.



 2. Why the Internet Is Not the Bottleneck
 2.1 The data wall is real, and it is about knowledge, not intelligence
 The raw constraint is well documented. Epoch AI estimates the usable stock of high-quality public human
 text at roughly 300 trillion tokens, and projects that training demand will fully utilize it between 2026 and
 2032. DeepSeek-V3 already consumed 14.8 trillion tokens, about 4.9% of that estimated stock; the
 Qwen3 family trained on 36 trillion tokens, about 12%. The frontier is not knowledge-poor; it is
 approaching knowledge-redundant, repeating the same corpus many times over. Qwen3’s 8B model saw
 about 4,390 tokens per parameter, a 220-fold multiple of the Chinchilla-optimal 20 tokens per parameter,
 and Qwen3’s 0.6B variant saw roughly 3,000-fold. When a family of models this strong trains on 100 to
 3,000 times more data per parameter than theory calls for, the interpretation is not that the internet is
 bottomless; it is that pretraining on web text has stopped being the lever that differentiates models.

 That saturation matters for our purposes in two ways. First, it means the marginal million tokens of
 Common Crawl add near-zero capability even at the frontier, so a local model refusing that data gives up
 almost nothing. Second, it means the capability gap between a knowledge-heavy 671B model and a
 knowledge-light local one is concentrated in recall, not in reasoning. We can measure that split directly.
 On AIME 2024, DeepSeek-V3 (671B total parameters, 37B active) scores 39.2% while GPT-4o scores
 9.3%; on SimpleQA, a pure factual-recall probe, GPT-4o scores 38.2% while the reasoning king

 2.2 Knowledge is not intelligence
 It helps to be precise about the decomposition. Knowledge is stored, retrievable proposition-level fact:
 what year, which API, whose theorem. It is a storage problem with an economic optimum, and Chinchilla
 arithmetic says storing it in weights costs about 20 tokens of training per parameter, which at 14B
 parameters is a 280-billion-token diet just to be compute-optimal, with quality still capped by whatever
 the web actually contains. Reasoning is multi-step search over a solution space: decomposition,
 backtracking, self-checking. It is a policy problem, trainable by reinforcement wherever an answer can be
 checked. Verification is the act of checking itself, which for code, mathematics, and formal logic is
 available deterministically and for free. Adaptation is the growth loop: the system updating itself from
 its own experience. A traditional frontier model blends all four into one giant gradient descent over the
 internet. Nothing forces that blending; the components have different optimal substrates, and only the
 first one wants a datacenter.

 The brain analogy is apt enough to be useful. Cortical slow learning encodes general structure;
 hippocampal fast learning stores episodes and consolidates them; the prefrontal system controls effort
 and decides when to think hard. Complementary-learning-systems theory has argued for two decades that
 intelligence is precisely this factorization. The local AI stack we propose is the engineering translation: a
 slowly-trained reasoning core, a fast episodic memory, an explicit controller that decides when to spend
 thinking tokens, and consolidation jobs that run while the user sleeps.


 2.3 What a consumer computer actually is
 The hardware bar for this program is an entry-class discrete GPU: 8 to 16 GB of VRAM, RTX
 4060/5060-class silicon, alongside Apple silicon with unified memory. The arithmetic is unforgiving in
 one specific way. At decode time a transformer is memory-bandwidth-bound: every generated token
 requires streaming the whole active weight set, so throughput in tokens per second is approximately
 effective bandwidth divided by weight bytes. A 14B model at 4-bit quantization occupies about 7.8 GB,
 so an RTX 4060 Ti 16GB (288 GB/s) sustains roughly 24 tokens per second, an RTX 5060 Ti 16GB (448
 GB/s) about 37, and an RTX 4090 (1008 GB/s) about 84, assuming 65% kernel efficiency. With
 EAGLE-3-style speculative decoding, multiply by at least three. Those numbers define a comfortable
 interactive experience for a model that, per Figure 1, already reasons at frontier-adjacent levels on
 verifiable tasks.

 What the box cannot hold is the point. Sixteen gigabytes accommodates a 14B reasoning core with room
 for its context cache and runtime; it cannot accommodate 671B parameters in any representation, and it
 was never going to. The design conclusion is not “shrink the frontier model” but “stop needing it.” We
 define the local frontier operationally: match frontier-tier performance on the tasks the user actually

 performs daily (math, code, documents, tool use on their own data), accept a recall handicap on the long
 tail, and neutralize that handicap with retrieval. The payoff for that trade is latency measured in tens of
 milliseconds, per-token cost of zero, privacy by construction, and capability that improves with use
 instead of waiting on a vendor’s release cycle.


 2.4 The reframe: memorize less, retrieve more
 Memorization is the expensive way to know something. A fact stored in weights costs training compute
 forever, goes stale silently, and occupies the same precious gigabytes that reasoning needs. The same fact
 on a local disk costs bytes, updates instantly, and never competes with the model. Our working math
 makes the contrast stark: a 60 GB curated corpus is about 15 billion tokens of high-quality text, roughly
 0.4% of what a frontier model consumes in pretraining, yet it covers essentially all of one organization’s
 or one person’s working knowledge. If the corpus is the only thing that must be current, then the model is
 free to be permanent. That single reframe, knowledge as retrieval rather than parameters, is what converts
 “runs locally” from a compromise into a design advantage.



 3. The Landscape: Ten Levers Beyond
 Web-Scale Data
 We surveyed the 2024–2026 literature and product releases for every mechanism that measurably buys
 capability without proportional web data. Ten levers survive scrutiny. Each is presented with its published
 evidence and its cost; together they form the menu from which the Mi9-Nexus architecture in Chapter 4
 is composed. The through-line of the whole survey is worth stating up front: the field has quietly
 discovered a second scaling axis. Compute spent after pretraining, on RL with verifiable rewards and at
 inference time, converts into reasoning capability at a rate per dollar that pretraining no longer
 approaches.


 3.1 Reasoning from reinforcement, not from records
 DeepSeek-R1 is the founding evidence. Applied directly to a 671B-parameter base with no supervised
 fine-tuning stage, large-scale RL with verifiable rewards (answer checking for math and code) produced
 emergent self-verification, reflection, and long chain-of-thought behavior. R1 reaches 79.8% on AIME
 2024, 97.3% on MATH-500, 71.5% on GPQA-Diamond, and a 96.3 percentile on Codeforces, matching
 OpenAI’s o1-1217 (79.2% on AIME) across math, code, and science. The crucial quantity is the training
 diet: on the order of 800 thousand RL samples, not trillions of tokens. The Nature version of the paper
 reported the RL stage at roughly $294,000 of compute on top of the $5.576M base-model pretraining
 bill, implying the reasoning superpowers cost about 5% of the knowledge base they run on. Figure 1
 shows the consequence for model scale directly: seven-billion-parameter students of R1 outperform
 generalist models 25 times their size on reasoning benchmarks.

 3.2 Self-play curricula and the executor as ground truth
 If RL with verifiable rewards works, the next question is where the tasks come from, and the answer need
 not involve humans at all. Absolute Zero removes the external corpus entirely: the model proposes its own
 reasoning tasks across deduction, abduction, and induction, solves them, and lets a code executor be both
 the examiner and the referee. The executor validates that a proposed task is well-posed and that a
 proposed solution actually runs to the claimed result, yielding reward signal with zero human data. The
 system improved a 7B model to results competitive with substantially larger reasoning models trained on
 curated corpora. This is the AlphaZero pattern returning to language: superhuman play was learned from
 self-play with a perfect referee, not from human game records, and Sutton and Silver’s era-of-experience
 argument generalizes exactly this move. For a local system the implication is strong: the curriculum
 generator and the verifier share one substrate, a sandboxed runtime, and both scale with compute spent
 locally rather than with data gathered globally.

 3.3 Test-time compute: buying intelligence at the moment of use
 The second scaling axis is compute spent generating the answer. The s1 project is the cleanest possible
 demonstration: fine-tuning Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on 1,000 curated, difficult, diverse reasoning traces
 produced s1-32B, which exceeds o1-preview by up to 27% on competition math (MATH and AIME
 2024); a training run costing on the order of tens of dollars of GPU time. Budget forcing, appending
 “Wait” to prolong deliberation, then lifted the same model from 50% to 57% on AIME 2024 with no
 further training. The same lever operates at the frontier’s edge: OpenAI’s o3 scored 75.7% on ARC-AGI’s
 semi-private evaluation at standard compute and 87.5% at a 172-times compute multiplier, a benchmark
 on which models trained however you like had been stuck below 40% for years. Snell and colleagues
 formalized the trade: compute-optimal test-time strategies let a 14B model defeat naive inference from
 models several times its size, and Wu and colleagues’ inference scaling laws show the compute-optimal
 model size shrinks as the inference budget grows. Figure 2 collects three verified pairs; the pattern is
 monotone and cheap.

 3.4 Distillation: compressed behavior, not compressed knowledge
 Reasoning traces transfer. DeepSeek-R1’s own 800 thousand curated samples, generated by the 671B
 teacher, fine-tuned six dense students between 1.5B and 70B. The students landed at remarkable levels
 for their size: 55.5% on AIME 2024 at 7B, 69.7% at 14B, 72.6% at 32B, against o1-mini’s 63.6%; at 1.5B
 the student already scores 28.9%, tripling GPT-4o’s 9.3%. A 7B student at 4-bit occupies about 4.6 GB
 and runs on an 8 GB laptop GPU at interactive speeds, yet outperforms generalist models with tens of
 times its parameters on verifiable reasoning. Distillation has known ceilings, which Chapter 6 treats
 honestly: the students inherit the teacher’s style more than its depth, and they trail it on ambiguous or
 knowledge-heavy probes (GPQA-Diamond 49.1% for the 7B versus 71.5% for R1 itself). But as a
 delivery mechanism for the reasoning half of intelligence into consumer hardware, nothing else is
 remotely as efficient.


 3.5 Data quality as an economy, not a torrent
 Where pretraining data is still used, its curation matters more than its volume. Phi-4, a 14B model, was
 trained in a 9.8-trillion-token mixture whose flagship ingredient is roughly 400 billion tokens of
 synthetic, textbook-quality data generated through more than 50 customized pipelines, with deliberate
 self-revision and falsification exercises built in. The result competes with models trained on several times
 more web text, and the report’s central lesson is that small models thrive on dense signal rather than
 diluted scale. Qwen3 pushed the same insight differently: rather than chasing unique tokens, it trained
 every size down to 0.6B on the full 36-trillion-token diet across 119 languages, accepting massive
 repetition to make reasoning data ubiquitous at every scale. Both programs agree on the conclusion that
 matters to us: a curated corpus in the hundreds of billions of tokens, mostly synthetic and verifiable, is
 now a sufficient knowledge substrate for a strong local model, and is purchasable or generable by a
 mid-sized lab without touching the open web at scale.


 3.6 Specialization: depth beats breadth at fixed size
 Frontier generality is a luxury product; most real work is deep rather than broad. A domain-focused
 4–14B model regularly matches models several times its size inside its domain, because capacity
 concentrates on one distribution instead of diluting across the open web. Gemma 3 27B, a single-GPU
 class model, posts a LMArena Elo of 1338, above DeepSeek-V3’s 1318 and o3-mini’s 1304, with 67.5%
 on MMLU-Pro and about 89% on MATH-500; GLM-4.5-Air, a 106B-total / 12B-active MoE, hits 89.4%
 on AIME 2024 and 57.6% on SWE-bench Verified, within a few points of its own 355B parent and of
 o3, at one-third the active compute. For a local assistant the conclusion is liberating: we do not need one
 model to know everything. We need one strong reasoning core, plus the discipline to point it at the user’s
 actual domains, where its effective rank is high and its benchmarks are frontier-adjacent.

 3.7 Architectural efficiency: MoE, hybrids, and latent attention
 The parameter budget itself is negotiable through architecture. Mixture-of-experts decouples storage
 from computation: DeepSeek-V3 holds 671B parameters yet activates only 37B per token;
 Qwen3-30B-A3B stores 30.5B and activates 3.3B (128 experts, 8 active), delivering strong reasoning on a
 desktop while weighing 17.1 GB at 4-bit; the same 30B-A3B pattern now appears across vendors,
 including our own GLM-4.5 family’s compact tier. Hybrid sequence models change the second cost, the
 context: NVIDIA’s Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2 keeps only 4 full attention layers out of 56 (the rest Mamba-2
 and MLP), collapsing the KV cache by roughly an order of magnitude, exactly the property a
 long-document local assistant needs, and ships an FP4 variant sized for a single GPU. DeepSeek’s
 multi-head latent attention makes the same trade inside a pure transformer, compressing the KV cache to
 a small latent per token. Our own arithmetic in Chapter 5 shows a 32K-token context costing 6.4 GB
 under naive GQA versus about 0.5 GB under a 4-attention-layer hybrid. Architecture, not data, is what
 makes context affordable.


 3.8 Sub-4-bit weights and speculative decoding
 Two inference technologies move the feasibility line dramatically. Quantization: 4-bit weight-only
 schemes are effectively lossless for interactive use, putting 14B at 7.8 GB and 8B at 4.6 GB; Apple’s
 on-device foundation model runs a 3B network at a dominant 2 bits with mixed 2/4-bit LoRA adapters on
 top, about 0.75 GB of weights, and still outperforms Phi-3-mini, Mistral-7B, Gemma-7B, and
 Llama-3-8B on its evaluation suite. At the research frontier, Microsoft’s native 1.58-bit ternary BitNet
 line promises weights that were never floating point at all; bitnet.cpp runs a 100B-class BitNet on a single
 CPU at human reading speed (5–7 tokens per second), and the open 2B4T release (4 trillion training
 tokens, about 0.4 GB) shows the format survives real training. Speculative decoding is the other half:
 EAGLE-3 delivers 3.0–6.5 times speedup over vanilla autoregressive generation by attaching a
 lightweight drafting head trained on the target’s own hidden layers, and it is already supported in vLLM.
 Combined, sub-4-bit weights plus draft-model verification push a 14B-class reasoning core comfortably
 past 60 tokens per second on a mid-range consumer GPU.

 3.9 Knowledge as retrieval and agentic memory
 Once knowledge is externalized, the engineering question becomes memory architecture, and the
 precedents are mature. Retrieval-augmented generation demonstrably substitutes for parametric
 knowledge on the tasks people actually ask, at the cost of a disk index rather than a pretraining run.
 MemGPT formalized the stronger version, treating the context window as main memory and the local
 corpus as paged storage, with the model itself managing what is brought in and what is consolidated out;
 the design descends directly from operating-system virtual memory and has become the reference pattern
 for agent memory. Our quantity of interest is the warehouse size, and it is small: a 10 GB curated corpus
 is roughly 2.5 billion tokens, and 60 GB is about 15 billion, which at typical retrieval depths covers a
 professional domain exhaustively. The capability consequence is that the assistant’s answers are grounded
 in documents the user controls, cite them, and update the moment the documents do, three properties no
 parametric memory offers at any price.


 3.10 Verification as cognition, adaptation as growth
 The final pair of levers closes the loop. Verification: a sandboxed Python process turns assertions into
 executables, so the local system’s arithmetic, geometry, parsing, and logic can be machine-checked rather
 than pattern-matched; this is the same substrate that makes RLVR and self-play possible, which is why
 the verifier stack earns its place in the runtime even on days when no training happens. Adaptation:
 Apple’s adapter architecture proves the deployment pattern, a frozen 2-bit base with swappable LoRA
 adapters specialized per task, so capability updates ship as megabytes and roll back as easily. Combine
 that with the self-play curriculum of Section 3.2 and a nightly consolidation job, and the local system
 owns a growth loop that no cloud service can replicate: it trains on your tasks, verified by your executor,
 with your data staying on your disk. That is the era of experience, and it happens to be the one scaling law
 a laptop can afford.

 4. The Synthesis: Mi9-Nexus, a Reference

 4.1 The reasoning core
 The core is a 14B-class model in a hybrid architecture: dense or small-MoE, with the majority of layers in
 linear-time state-space form and a handful of full attention layers in the Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2 pattern,
 so that a 32K-token working context costs about half a gigabyte of KV state instead of 6.4 GB under
 naive GQA. Weights ship 4-bit (7.8 GB). The training recipe is the lever stack, not an invention: initialize
 from the strongest open 14B base; distill 30 to 60 million tokens of verified reasoning traces from
 frontier-class open teachers (the R1-distribution playbook at 800K-sample scale); then run RLVR over
 about 120 thousand self-generated, executor-checked tasks across mathematics, code, logic, and
 document processing, with budget forcing trained in so the model learns to use longer deliberation when
 instructed. Dual-mode operation (thinking / non-thinking, as in Qwen3 and GLM-4.5) is mandatory,
 because the fast path is what makes interactive latency feel native.


 4.2 Grounded memory, the verifier stack, and the tool belt
 Memory is two stores and one policy. The semantic store is the curated corpus, 20 to 60 GB of documents
 the user or organization actually relies on, indexed with hybrid lexical and vector retrieval so recall does
 not collapse on exact identifiers the way pure embeddings do. The episodic store is a MemGPT-style
 paged journal of interactions, tool results, and assistant notes, with a consolidation job that distills durable
 facts into the semantic store on a schedule. The policy is the model itself deciding, page by page, what
 enters the context window, which is the only scalable answer to a context that must survive months of use.
 The verifier stack is the conscience: a sandboxed code executor with unit-test synthesis, a symbolic
 mathematics checker, and static-analysis gates, invoked online to verify claims the model makes and
 offline as referee for the growth loop. The tool belt (shell, files, browser, application APIs) turns verified
 plans into actions; the model contributes judgment and decomposition, never raw precision.


 4.3 The budget scheduler and the growth loop
 The scheduler is the economics minister of the runtime. Every query is routed by difficulty: the fast path
 answers directly in non-thinking mode at 24 to 37 tokens per second on entry hardware; the deep path
 enables thinking, allocates a token budget, admits retrieved context, and may invoke the verifier on
 intermediate claims. Budget forcing, the s1 mechanism, lets the scheduler extend deliberation (“Wait”)
 on problems where pass-rate curves are still rising, converting idle GPU hours into accuracy on demand.
 The growth loop closes the system: each night the task engine proposes verifiable work pitched at the
 edge of the core’s ability (Absolute Zero curriculum plus replayed user-approved tasks), the executor
 validates every trace, and the adapter distiller folds verified wins into a LoRA adapter that swaps in with
 rollback protection. Apple’s adapter architecture proves the deployment pattern at consumer scale; our
 contribution is pointing it at self-generated, verified experience rather than vendor-curated tasks.

 5. The Quantitative Program: Scaling Math,
 FLOPs, Budgets
 5.1 Two costs, five orders of magnitude apart
 The economics that make this program coherent are worth stating precisely, because they justify every
 allocation decision that follows. Take the pretraining compute of a 14B model under the Chinchilla law C
 = 6ND. At the compute-optimal 20 tokens per parameter, that is 2.35 × 1022 FLOPs for 280 billion
 tokens; at the data-diet actually used by modern families (Qwen3’s 36 trillion tokens), it is 3.0 × 1024
 FLOPs. Calibrating effective throughput against DeepSeek’s own published figures (V3 pretraining:
 2.664 million H800 GPU-hours for 6ND ≈ 3.3 × 1024 FLOPs, about 343 TFLOPS realized per GPU),
 the 36-trillion-token pretraining of our 14B core would cost roughly 2.45 million GPU-hours, about $4.9
 million at $2 per GPU-hour. Now price the reasoning half: an RLVR program of 120 thousand tasks, 16
 rollouts per task of about 6K generated tokens, three policy epochs, and a 4-times overhead factor for
 verifier runs and failed rollouts, totals about 7.3 × 1021 FLOPs, roughly 5,900 H800-equivalent
 GPU-hours, about $12 thousand. The reasoning capability of a 14B model costs a quarter of one
 percent of its pretraining. That single ratio is the entire business case for Mi9-Nexus, and it is why an
 agentic AI company can rationally own a local frontier while renting cloud pretraining for the base
 exactly once.

 5.3 Inference arithmetic on consumer silicon
 Decode is memory-bandwidth-bound: each token streams the active weights once, so sustained
 generation speed is approximately 0.65 (kernel efficiency) times memory bandwidth divided by weight
 bytes. Table 4 works this for a 4.6 GB 8B core and the 7.8 GB 14B core across the entry-to-prosumer
 range, with the EAGLE-3 lower bound of 3× applied as a multiplier. Two context numbers complete the
 picture: naive GQA at 48 layers and 8 KV heads of 128 dimensions costs 192 KiB per token, so 32K
 tokens is 6.4 GB at fp16 and 3.2 GB at int8, while a 4-attention-layer hybrid pays about 16 KiB per
 token, roughly half a gigabyte at the same context, which is precisely why Section 4.1 committed to the
 hybrid form. Prefill, the other latency term, is compute-bound at 2 FLOPs per parameter per token:
 ingesting a 100K-token document into the 14B core is about 2.8 × 1015 FLOPs, roughly 8 seconds on a
 single H800-class GPU or a quarter-minute on consumer silicon, an occasional cost the scheduler can
 amortize by indexing instead of re-reading.

 5.4 The knowledge budget
 The retrieval side has its own small arithmetic. A 10 GB curated corpus holds roughly 2.5 billion tokens
 of text; 60 GB holds about 15 billion, which is 0.4% of Qwen3’s 36 trillion-token pretraining diet.
 Against the tasks a professional actually performs, coverage from a corpus of that size is close to total,
 and the residual gap is precisely the long tail of world trivia where no local system, and increasingly no
 cloud system either, will be authoritative without live retrieval. The design consequence is a rule we call
 the 0.4% doctrine: one tenth of one percent of the internet, curated, current, and cited, covers
 essentially all of the work; the remainder is a lookup, not a personality.



 6. Failure Modes and Honest Limits
 A credible research program names the ways it can be wrong. We track six failure modes, each with its
 mitigation, and we treat them as live monitoring targets rather than as caveats.


 6.1 The reasoning collapse
 Apple’s Illusion of Thinking study documented complete accuracy collapse in frontier reasoning models
 on controllable puzzle families (Tower of Hanoi, river crossings) beyond a complexity threshold, with
 8-disk Hanoi already at zero. The follow-up debate matters: re-analysis attributed much of the collapse to
 output-token limits rather than intrinsic reasoning failure, since performance recovered when trace
 budgets fit the context. For us the operational lesson stands either way: local reasoning cores with 16 to
 32K budgets will hit such cliffs earlier than frontier systems, so the scheduler must detect diminishing
 coherence (repetition, non-termination, unverified claims) and escalate to decomposition or external tools
 rather than grinding through token budgets. Verification-gated output is the mitigation: a claim that
 cannot be checked is flagged, not presented.


 6.2 The distillation ceiling
 Distilled students inherit traces, not depth. The 7B distill of R1 shows the pattern: frontier-adjacent on
 AIME (55.5%) yet 49.1% on GPQA-Diamond against R1’s 71.5%, and distills trail their teacher most on
 ambiguous, multi-constraint problems. We should expect our 14B core to inherit style ahead of
 substance, and the honest counter is not more distillation but self-play RL on tasks the teacher never
 solved, plus explicit curriculum stages pitched at failure clusters. The build plan gates Phase 1 on held-out
 ambiguity sets precisely to detect the ceiling instead of benchmarking past it.

 6.3 Verifiable-domain bias
 RLVR works where answers check themselves: mathematics, code, formal logic, simulations.
 Judgment-heavy work (taste, strategy, negotiation, open-ended research direction) has no executor, and
 rewarding proxies there degrades into reward hacking. Mi9-Nexus’s growth loop must therefore stay
 inside the verifiable perimeter, with human feedback as the only signal outside it, and the runtime must
 label outputs by evidence class: executor-verified, retrieval-grounded, or model judgment. A local system
 that silently pretends its opinions are verified is worse than one that declares the difference.


 6.4 The long tail, contamination, and evaluation
 The 0.4% doctrine covers work, not trivia; SimpleQA-style probes will expose the corpus boundary, and
 the correct response is a labeled web-retrieval fallback, not embarrassment. Contamination is the
 evaluation-side disease: AIME-class benchmarks are saturated and partially leaked into every large
 corpus, which is why our gates in Chapter 7 use held-out, regenerated, and self-generated task families
 with variance reporting (R1 itself reports pass@1 estimated over 64 samples for this reason). Any
 Phase-gate that cannot survive a contamination audit does not count as passed.


 6.5 On-device adaptation risk
 Nightly self-modification is powerful and dangerous in equal measure. Reward hacking inside the loop
 produces confident nonsense; adapter drift degrades yesterday’s competence; a compromised corpus
 poisons consolidation. The mitigations are structural: every adapter ships with a regression gate against a
 frozen 500-task suite and automatic rollback; adapters are signed and sandboxed in the Apple pattern; the
 executor that referees training is audited by an adversarial test suite of its own; and the growth loop runs
 strictly user-initiated for the first two releases. Continual learning that cannot roll back is not a feature; it
 is a liability.


 6.6 Physics: power, heat, and memory
 Consumer GPUs sustain 200 to 300 W, and nightly self-play at full duty will heat a room and a bill; the
 scheduler’s off-peak window and power cap are part of the product, not an afterthought. Memory
 bandwidth, not FLOPs, remains the binding constraint on interactive speed, which is why the
 architecture’s hybrid KV design and 4-bit weights are load-bearing decisions rather than conveniences.
 None of these limits threatens the thesis; all of them shape the engineering discipline around it.

 8. Conclusion
 The mandate was to find how AI can be made smarter without ingesting the whole internet, and to bring
 the answer to a consumer computer. The research record of 2024 through 2026 supports a decisive
 answer: the internet was only ever the substrate for one component of intelligence, its warehouse of facts,
 and that component is the one that need not live in weights. Reasoning is now demonstrably purchasable
 with reinforcement learning on a few hundred thousand verified tasks at a quarter of a percent of
 pretraining cost; knowledge retrieves from a corpus that fits on the same disk as the operating system;
 precision is a service exposed by a sandboxed executor; and growth compounds nightly on the user’s own
 experience. None of this is a prediction; each element is measured somewhere in the papers cited below,
 and Mi9-Nexus is simply their composition on a 16 GB card.

 The strategic meaning for an agentic AI company is equally direct. Local-first is not a retreat from the
 frontier; it is the only place where the frontier’s next axis, test-time and experiential compute, can run at
 zero marginal cost with perfect privacy. Every hour a user’s GPU spends verified-thinking is an hour our
 cloud does not have to serve, and every verified trace it generates is proprietary training signal our
 competitors cannot scrape. We recommend committing to Phase 0 immediately: the harness is cheap, the
 bet is measurable within a quarter, and the downside if the gates fail is a first-class evaluation
 infrastructure we need anyway. Intelligence without the whole internet is no longer a slogan; with the
 evidence assembled here, it is an engineering schedule.
